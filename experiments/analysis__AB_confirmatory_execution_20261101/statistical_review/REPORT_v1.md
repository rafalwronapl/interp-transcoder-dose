## Findings

**SR1 high, open — crossed stability intervals lack adequate coverage.** The current 95% percentile crossed bootstrap with 8 independent FIT cohorts covered the population mean only 0.876 (normal) and 0.878 (t3) in 500 independent FIT-dominant simulations; MC SE approximately0.0147. A second stress screen with8FIT×32TEST gave0.868/0.884. Merely constructing99% intervals with8FIT reached0.938/0.934 in this stress case, which does not certify95%coverage. With16FIT,99%construction reached0.970normal/0.982t3 in the same screen; additive cases0.990/0.988, interaction cases1.000. This is a candidate fix, not final proof. The evidence is crossed_coverage.json and crossed_stress_screen.json, with reproducible audit scripts. These simulations show why TEST-only or flattened-row inference is invalid, and why the number of FIT cohorts needs its own calibration. Keep8FIT purely descriptive if retained, or choose16/cohort alternatives and a calibrated interval using predeclared acceptance criteria. The independent primary r0 experiment is not statistically invalidated by this separate-module issue.

**SR2 high, open — final calibration does not yet cover the least favorable composite nulls.** The A screen tests one partial-null configuration. The B partial-null screen leaves all three family hypotheses null, with one component per IUT positive. Add both orientations of each IUT boundary, single-null configurations with the other two hypotheses strongly positive, and different true-null subsets. Holm becomes less stringent after true alternatives are rejected. Exercise dependence and sign/correlation regimes, finite-variance heavy-tailed positive energies, and missing/zero cells in the full family pipeline. The existing screen labels are appropriately provisional. B2 ratio algebra looks sound; its simulation is not yet a complete family calibration.

**SR3 high, open — final N/precision decision is unimplemented and unfrozen.** Before PILOT, materialize sample_size_spec with effect scales, CI construction, alpha allocation, candidate/maxN, PILOT variance rule, pilot-variance uncertainty, power/precision acceptance and infeasibility outcome. The proposed A L=0.04logit² and B halfwidth0.10logit/0.02relativeRMS can be operational scientific goals when justified and explicitly separated from support thresholds. V target0.05×meanFIT_U||D||² is a defensible scale convention if its formula is frozen beforeFIT, its numeric realization frozen beforePILOT, and raw V remains the analysis estimand. Twelve B precision outputs means2operators×(so,when,equal mixture)×(absolute RMS,relative RMS). Bonferroni intervals across12 are valid conservatively, but90%assurance must require simultaneous attainment of all12 precision goals, not12 separate90%assurances. There is no implemented RMS/relativeRMS CI or assurance simulation in the reviewed code yet. A standardized0.4 synthetic alternative alone does not validate power at the proposed physical-unit effects. Similarly,8 or16FIT is not selected by current simulations for an explicit precision goal.

**SR4 medium, resolved in current source — empty FIT could previously produce NaNs.** An empty(0,12) input returned scaleNaN and NaN intercept rather than stopping. The implementation now checks nonempty positive dimensions, positive finite regularization, finite positive inputRMS and finite fitted coefficients. Independent rerun rejects this example and all8tests pass. Source manifest binds the corrected version. Add an explicit empty-FIT regression test before production integration.

**SR5 medium, open — Monte Carlo acceptance rules must quantify uncertainty.** Report binomial confidence bounds for FWER, coverage, power and joint precision assurance, with a prespecified tolerance and replication count. The screen's plug-in SE is useful but equals0 when all500replicates pass; this is not proof of power/coverage1. A one-sided Wilson/Clopper–Pearson bound or another fixed method should govern acceptance, including multiple simulation scenarios. Using20,000bootstrap draws in production with999in screening is acceptable for a first screen; final calibration should establish that changing resample count does not alter conclusions near rejection boundaries.

Pointwise95%intervals are not simultaneous precision bounds and need not be dual to Holm decisions.
