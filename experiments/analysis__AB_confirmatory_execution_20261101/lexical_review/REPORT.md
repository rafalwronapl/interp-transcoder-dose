# Independent lexical design review — 2026-11-01

Reviewer: `/root/sol_ab_execution/lexical_review`. Offline lexical inspection only; no pretrained models loaded. This review does not authorize a launch and does not approve a final corpus.

The raw artifact pool is unsuitable as a certified regular concrete-count-noun corpus. The 2,919 entries include abstract or mass readings. They form 2,385 connected components under shared noun synsets; 464 synsets contain multiple aliases. Eight hundred ten candidate forms have a noun synonym found in the historical observed-word inventory. Literal lemma freshness does not itself require semantic synonym disjointness, but alternate spellings should not be sold as independent semantic replication.

`morphy(word + 's') == word` is not a valid regular-plural validator: it accepts, for example, *cragsmans*. A conservative filter must also reject singular values in WordNet's noun exception map when any recorded plural differs from `word + 's'`. We additionally screened endings man/woman/person/child. This sacrifices valid alternative regular forms but avoids relying on WordNet's generic suffix fallback.

Expanding to first noun senses denoting people and individual animals is numerically feasible. The review screen in `expanded_pool_audit_v4.json` has 2,359 representatives, sufficient for the proposed 1,344 positions before broader exposure checks. Its stages were: regular candidate form; first noun sense person/animal or prior artifact-descendant pool; definition begins a/an/someone/one who; no noun-synonym overlap with historical observed words; one representative per connected candidate noun-synset component; first sense descends from animal.n.01/person.n.01/instrumentality.n.03/structure.n.01/container.n.01/covering.n.02; original first-sense lemma spelling is lowercase; exclude -ing forms and material/substance/fabric/toiletry ancestors; reject recorded irregular plurals and man/woman/person/child endings. Representative selection prefers the first-sense synset headword, then shortest spelling, then lexical order. Filtering occurs after this choice and therefore conservatively discards some otherwise viable groups.

The hash-selected manual sample of 80 at v3 mostly denotes countable entities; retained sample words are listed in v4 after irregular exclusion. It exposed mass *brilliantine*, taxonomic family/genus names, capitalized nationality names and irregular compounds during earlier screens; v4 removes these categories. No claim is made that every retained word was manually validated. The remaining pool contains rare, archaic, occupational and sometimes derogatory senses. A study using it estimates performance on this machine-defined WordNet population, rather than on familiar everyday nouns. Tokenization length and model competence need descriptive reporting and the already authorized disjoint PILOT; TEST rows cannot later be removed because unfamiliar words perform poorly.

The 904-file historical inventory is incomplete even for lexical material. A filename search for prompts/documents/items/general_text and related labels found 227 further files. One genuine previous pilot, `analysis/SECOND_LOOK_20261023/A0b_geometry/preparation/v3/pilot_documents_v1.json`, contains *bassoonist*, present in the new screened pool. The additional list and hashes are in `unlabelled_source_audit.json`; include these sources and redo literal/plural/synonym exposure checks before selection. Many of the 227 files are technical fixtures or reviewer prompts, so scanning them all is conservative. The file `runs/E006/items.json` and `analysis/TC_EXTENDED_20261030/general_text.json` are also outside the initial 904 set. A wider inventory should inspect dataset readers for other unlabelled inputs; this review does not establish exhaustive repository coverage.
